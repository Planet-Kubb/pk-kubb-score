The grammar

A precise definition of what counts as Planet Kubb Notation, written so that a computer can check it.

The notation page explains the notation to people. A formal grammar explains it to machines: a short set of rules that decides, for any text at all, whether it is valid notation, and if it is, exactly what each piece means. No judgment calls, no "it depends on context". Because the rules are exact, anyone can build a parser in any language and it will read every game the same way.

This grammar is also a program. The same file that defines the notation is compiled into the parser this site uses, and it is tested against every recorded game.

Reading the rules

Each rule below is drawn as a railroad diagram. Start at the left and follow the track to the right. Whenever the track splits you can take either branch, and loops let you repeat. Any path you can trace spells out something valid. Green boxes are literal characters to type. Brown boxes are other rules, drawn further down the page.

A turn

A turn line, and then any position lines that belong to it.

The turn line

One line: an optional turn number, the body, then an optional # note.

The body of a turn

The phases always come in order: kubbs thrown in, then the advantage line, then the batons. Any of them can be left out, but there has to be at least one. This is how the grammar knows 2I A F is valid and F 2I isn't.

Kubbs thrown in

An optional player, then either a plain number (3 means three kubbs) or kubb tossing codes, each with its own count, like 5I2R. An E on the end means a thrown kubb knocked over the king.

The advantage line

A with an optional distance of 0 to 4 meters, or one of the 2012 markers BA, MA and CA.

A baton

An optional player, then what the baton did. Field kubbs come before base kubbs, so 2FB is valid and B2F isn't.

Separators

A space, a comma, a forward slash or a comment in parentheses.

Headers

Position lines

Notation 2 adds lines that say where things were on the pitch. Each sits under its turn line and starts with a mark and a half: @ for where the field kubbs stand, ~ for where the thrown kubbs lay, ^ for where each baton was thrown from, and * for which kubbs each baton felled. The notation page explains them.

The pitch line

Every field kubb standing in one half. Kubbs in the same cell take a count, and a letter says how a kubb came to be there: p for a punishment kubb, s for one left from an earlier turn.

The lay, stance and felled lines

Written by programs. The stance and felled lines have one word for each baton on the turn line.

A position

Across, a comma, depth. Whole numbers are a one-meter cell. Two decimals each are a point. The grammar holds both to the pitch: across 0 to 5 and depth 0 to 4.

The canonical form

The rules above are the lenient form, which reads what people write. The grammar has a second start rule, CanonicalGame, for what programs write: one spelling of each thing. A canonical file starts with [Notation "2"], has its headers in a fixed order, numbers its turns from 1, and puts exactly one space between tokens. Every canonical file is also valid in the lenient form, with the same reading.

Tokens are stricter too: a player always has its colon, a count of one is never written, and the kubb codes come in one order.

What the grammar settles

Writing the notation down this precisely forced some questions the prose never had to answer. Here is how the grammar answers them.

  • Case doesn't matter for actions: 2f and 2F are the same.
  • Counts run from 0 to 10. 11I is rejected. Advantage lines run from 0 to 4 meters, and neighbors from 0 to 5.
  • The colon after a player can be left out only when the player is lowercase and the action starts with a capital, a digit or a symbol. fB is player f knocking over a base kubb. FB and fb are a field kubb that took a base kubb, with no player.
  • Player tokens are letters, plus underscores. The underscore is there for one archived player id.
  • Every token must end at a separator, a comment, a note or the end of the line. So 32F is not "3, then 2F", and F- is an error, not "F, then a miss".
  • Comments don't nest, and they stay on one line.
  • Headers come before the turns, in PGN style: [Key "value"], with \" for a quote inside the value.
  • One turn line per turn. Blank lines and lines with only a # note can go anywhere.
  • A position line is all cells or all points, and a position off the pitch is rejected. Only the two ends of a lying kubb may run past a line, by up to 0.20 meters.
  • The lines of one turn have to agree. The kubbs marked p number the turn's P, and a stance or felled line has one word for each baton. The canonical form rejects a turn where they don't. The lenient form reads it and reports it.
  • A file that names a notation the grammar doesn't know is rejected, so a later notation is never misread as this one.
  • A bare N is one neighbor.

What the grammar doesn't check is whether a game adds up: the right number of kubbs thrown in, an advantage line when field kubbs were left standing, no king shot while kubbs are still standing. Those depend on everything that came before, so they're checked by replaying the game kubb by kubb. That's what the warnings on the Stats page are.

Tested against every recorded game

Of the 150 games recorded on the Planet Kubb wiki, 149 are valid. All 1,328 of their turns and 7,954 batons get exactly the same reading from the grammar as from the Stats page. The one rejected game is a real slip. In turn 6 of game 3 of the 2012 Nordic Fest final, one baton was written F-, and the grammar points at the stray -.

Before notation 2 changed anything, the reading of every one of those games was written down. The grammar is still held to it, along with a conformance suite of files that must be read a given way and files that must be rejected. Each of the 149 valid games can also be rewritten in the canonical form and plays out exactly as before.

The grammar itself

It's a parsing expression grammar (PEG), written for the Peggy parser generator, in about 650 lines including comments. The comments at the top explain how to read it. Download planet-kubb-notation.peggy
